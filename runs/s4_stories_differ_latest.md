# Which stopped trials in the snapshot have an 8-K that gives a different reason than the registry? Start with the ones checked by hand.

## Answer
Among the hand-checked stopped trials in the snapshot, NCT03980730, NCT05528861, and NCT02227784 each have 8-K filings that attribute termination to clinical efficacy failures, contrasting with their registries which cite strategic or business reasons. [1][2][3][4][5][6] The broader snapshot contains 43 trials labeled with a discrepancy between the registry reason and other disclosures, 5 of which were checked by hand. [7]

## Timeline
For NCT03980730, the trial started on 2019-06-27 and completed on 2021-01-29; results were due on 2021-12-01 and posted on 2022-01-21, with an 8-K filed on 2021-02-24 and no publication found. For NCT05528861, the trial started on 2022-10-26 and completed on 2024-04-18; results were due on 2024-12-26 and posted on 2024-09-27, with an 8-K filed on 2024-01-16 and no publication found. For NCT02227784, the trial started in 2014-10 and completed in 2015-12; results were due on 2016-11-30 and posted on 2018-03-22, with an 8-K filed on 2015-10-22 and a publication published on 2017-06-01.

## Evidence
- NCT03980730 was terminated with the registry reason "for business reasons", while its 8-K filed on 2021-02-24 stated the phase 2 study "did not meet its primary objective." [1][2]
- NCT05528861 was terminated with the registry reason "No longer pursuing development", while its 8-K filed on 2024-01-16 stated the study "Did Not Meet Their Primary Endpoints." [3][4]
- NCT02227784 was terminated with the registry reason "Study termination due to program termination.", while its 8-K filed on 2015-10-22 cited "insufficient efficacy." [5][6]
- The primary results of NCT02227784 were published in Atherosclerosis on 2017-06-01, noting findings that may contribute to a "lack of clinical benefit." [8]
- Out of 3,739 stopped trials in the broader snapshot, 1,488 were terminated for business or strategic reasons. [9]

## Where the stories differ
- For NCT03980730, there is a discrepancy between the registry reason of "for business reasons" and the 8-K disclosure that the study did not meet its primary objective. [1][2]
- For NCT05528861, there is a discrepancy between the registry reason "No longer pursuing development" and the 8-K statement that the drug did not meet its primary endpoints. [3][4]
- For NCT02227784, there is a discrepancy between the registry reason "Study termination due to program termination." and the 8-K which cites insufficient efficacy. [5][6][8]

## What the data can't tell us
- The data cannot tell us the real reason a trial was stopped beyond the text entered in the registry or SEC filings.
- A discrepancy between registry entries and SEC disclosures is not proof of intent to mislead.
- The results due date is an approximation of the FDAAA deadline, which only applies to certain applicable clinical trials, meaning a delay is not proof that a sponsor broke the law.
- Results published in conference abstracts, non-indexed journals, or company websites may have been missed by the search.
- Private sponsors do not file documents with the SEC, so their investor communications are not captured here.
