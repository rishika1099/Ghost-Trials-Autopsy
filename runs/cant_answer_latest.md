# Did Sorrento Therapeutics break the law by not posting results for its terminated COVID-19 trials?

## Answer
These public records cannot show whether anyone broke the law; they show only what was reported and when. The trials registry shows that both NCT04903327 and NCT04900428 are terminated and have no results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov. [1][2] Their estimated results due dates were 2023-08-29 and 2023-03-22, respectively, making them 1164 and 1324 days late. [1][2]

## Timeline
For trial NCT04903327, the trial started on 2021-11-16, reached primary completion on 2022-08-29, and has never posted results, with a calculated due date of 2023-08-29. For trial NCT04900428, the trial started on 2021-07-16, reached primary completion on 2022-03-22, and has never posted results, with a calculated due date of 2023-03-22.

## Evidence
- Trial NCT04903327 was started on 2021-11-16, reached primary completion on 2022-08-29, and was terminated due to slow enrollment. [1]
- Trial NCT04900428 was started on 2021-07-16, reached primary completion on 2022-03-22, and was terminated at an interim analysis because the drops did not work on new variants. [2]
- Neither trial has results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov, and PubMed lists no primary results publications for either study. [1][2][3][4]
- Sorrento's 8-K filings in 2023 mentioned the trademarks for COVI-MSC and COVI-DROPS but did not report that these trials had been terminated. [5][6][7]
- The 2021 10-K reported that phase II enrollment had begun for COVI-DROPS, but did not mention its termination. [8]
- The 2022 10-K and several 2022 10-Q filings continued to list the associated drug candidates as active clinical pipeline assets without disclosing the trial stoppages. [9][10][11]

## Where the stories differ
- The trials registry lists specific reasons for stopping both trials, but Sorrento's SEC filings did not disclose these terminations and continued to list the treatments as active pipeline assets. [1][2][9][10][11]

## What the data can't tell us
- We cannot tell the real reasons for stopping these trials beyond the brief explanations provided in the trials registry.
- A discrepancy between registry listings and investor reports is not proof of any intent to mislead.
- The calculated results due date (primary completion plus 365 days) is only an approximation of the FDAAA deadline, which only applies to "applicable clinical trials" whose status is not fully public, meaning this report is not evidence that a sponsor broke the law.
- Results published in conference abstracts, non-indexed journals, or company websites may have been missed by our search of PubMed and Europe PMC.
- Trials outside the snapshot scope were only looked up live, or not at all.
