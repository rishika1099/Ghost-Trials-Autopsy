# vTv Therapeutics terminated its phase 2 Elevage trial of azeliragon (NCT03980730). What reason did it give the registry, what did it tell investors, and do the two match?

## Answer
The registry record for the Phase 2 Elevage trial of azeliragon states that the trial was terminated for business reasons. [1] In contrast, vTv Therapeutics informed investors that the study was discontinued because it did not meet its primary objective. [2] These two reasons represent a discrepancy between a business strategic explanation and an efficacy failure. [1][2]

## Timeline
Trial NCT03980730 started on 2019-06-27 and reached primary completion on 2020-12-01. vTv Therapeutics announced the conclusion of enrollment in an 8-K filed on 2020-11-05, disclosed the trial's efficacy failure in an 8-K on 2021-02-24, and posted results to ClinicalTrials.gov on 2022-01-21 after the 2021-12-01 results due date.

## Evidence
- Trial NCT03980730 was terminated with an enrollment of 43 participants. [1]
- The ClinicalTrials.gov registry lists the why_stopped text as "for business reasons," which is classified under the business/strategic category. [1]
- The primary completion date was 2020-12-01, and results were posted to the registry on 2022-01-21, which was 51 days after the estimated results due date of 2021-12-01. [1]
- On 2020-11-05, the sponsor announced it had concluded enrollment in the study at the end of September and expected topline results in December. [3]
- On 2021-02-24, vTv Therapeutics reported to the SEC that the Elevage study did not meet its primary objective, resulting in the discontinuation of azeliragon for Alzheimer's disease. [2]
- The company also stated that it was evaluating potential alternative indications for the drug in partnership with other parties. [2]
- No direct peer-reviewed publication of the trial's results was located in PubMed or Europe PMC. [4][5][6][7][8]

## Where the stories differ
- The registry reason for termination is listed as business reasons, which does not match the SEC filing stating that the trial was stopped because it failed to meet its primary objective. [1][2]

## What the data can't tell us
- We cannot determine the real strategic or clinical reasons for stopping the trial beyond the brief texts provided by the sponsor.
- The discrepancy between the registry reason and the investor disclosure is not proof of an intent to mislead.
- Although results were posted after the estimated results due date, this date only approximates the FDAAA deadline, which applies to applicable clinical trials whose status is not fully public, meaning this is not proof that the sponsor broke the law.
- Our search might have missed results published in conference abstracts, non-indexed journals, or company websites.
